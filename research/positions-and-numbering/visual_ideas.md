# Visual ideas: positions and numbering

## Zone reveal pitch (Show, then Touch)

A bare pitch in Pitch Standard coordinates. Learner taps a zone and the position name, a one-line duty and a sample player silhouette fade in. Tap a second time to flip between "in possession" and "out of possession" duty. Keyboard: arrow keys move between zones.

## Number migration timeline (Show)

A scrubber from 1928 to the present with one pitch. At 1928 the eleven numbers sit on a 2-3-5. Scrub forward and the numbers glide to a 4-4-2 and then a 4-2-3-1, so the learner sees the 5 slide from the middle line into central defence and the 6 either follow or hold. A second track marks 1939 (compulsory) and 1993 (squad numbers), where the numbers stop mapping to places and become labels.

## Drag numbers onto the shape (Touch)

Eleven numbered tokens and an empty 2-3-5. Learner drags each number to where history put it. A button then morphs the shape to 4-4-2 and the learner must re-drag the 4, 5 and 6. A "country" switch (England, Argentina, Brazil) reshuffles the answer key and shows why the same number sits in different places.

## Position versus role sliders (Touch)

One full-back token at a fixed starting spot. Two toggles, "team has the ball" and "team does not", each show the token's heat zone. Switch the role between full-back and inverted full-back and watch only the in-possession zone move. The lesson point: the starting position is unchanged while the job is different.

## Squad-number rule checker (Practise)

A mini roster of a Premier League first-team squad. The learner assigns numbers and a rule panel lights up red or green for each Handbook rule: different number for each player, same number all season, one on the shirt back and one on the shorts. A late signing arrives mid-season and the learner must pick a free number. Plain-language rule text, with the rule number shown as a source tag.

## Label translator (Practise)

Two columns: a data provider position code (for example RCB, LWB, CAM) and a pitch. Learner drops each code on the right zone, then flips to a second provider scheme to see the same player under a different label. Ends with a "which labels are positions and which are roles" sort.

## Myth or mechanism quiz cards (Practise)

Short cards for "the number tells you the role" and "a 4-4-2 fixes everyone in place". Learner predicts the answer, then a tiny animation shows the counter-example, such as a number 7 who plays as a second striker or a full-back who steps into midfield.
