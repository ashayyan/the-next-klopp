# Positions and numbering

## Key concepts

A position is the pitch zone a player starts in and mainly works from, and positions are less rigidly fixed in football than in rugby or American football. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions#positions-not-rigid]
A team fields at most eleven players and one of them must be the goalkeeper. [src-ifab-laws-2026-27]
The goalkeeper works near their own goal, stops shots, organises the defence and starts attacks by distributing the ball. [src-bundesliga-positions-explained][src-wikipedia-association-football-positions]
Outside the penalty area the goalkeeper has the same handling restrictions as any other player. [src-ifab-laws-2026-27]
The goalkeeper's shirt and socks must be distinguishable from outfield players and match officials, and the Premier League Handbook repeats this as a kit rule. [src-ifab-laws-2026-27][src-premierleague-handbook-2026-27#m10-goalkeeper-kit]
Centre-backs play in the middle of defence in front of the goalkeeper, block attacks and clear danger. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions][src-bundesliga-positions-explained]
Full-backs guard the left and right flanks and mark opposing wingers, and modern full-backs also overlap to attack. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions][src-bundesliga-positions-explained]
A wing-back is an attack-minded wide defender, mostly seen in shapes with three centre-backs, and counts as part of midfield when the team attacks. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions]
A defensive midfielder sits in front of the defence and screens it with tackles and interceptions. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions][src-bundesliga-positions-explained]
A central midfielder links defence and attack and contributes in both phases. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions][src-bundesliga-positions-explained]
An attacking midfielder plays behind the striker, creates chances, and is traditionally called the number ten. [src-bundesliga-positions-explained][src-fourfourtwo-no10#ten-traditionally-attacking-mid]
A winger plays wide near the touchline and uses pace and dribbling to beat full-backs and deliver crosses. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions][src-bundesliga-positions-explained]
The centre-forward or striker plays closest to the opposition goal, scores, and holds the ball up for teammates. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions][src-bundesliga-positions-explained]
The position names grew out of the 2-3-5, where the back pair were full-backs, the middle three were half-backs and the front five were wingers, inside forwards and a centre-forward. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions]
The word half-back faded by the early 1970s and midfield took its place. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions]

## Must teach

Beginner map: one goalkeeper, then defenders (centre-back, full-back, wing-back), midfielders (defensive, central, attacking) and attackers (winger, centre-forward). [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions][src-bundesliga-positions-explained]
A centre-back's out-of-possession job is to read the game and take up position early. [src-thefa-central-defenders-bootroom#centre-back-read-the-game]
A position is where a player starts; a role is the job the team plan gives them from there. [src-fourfourtwo-inverted-full-back][src-fourfourtwo-no10]
Example of a role inside a position: an inverted full-back starts as a full-back but moves into central space when their team has the ball. [src-fourfourtwo-inverted-full-back#inverted-full-back-definition]
Example of position and role together: the number ten is an attacking midfield position, usually held by a playmaker, but a defensive player can be used there in a pressing job. [src-fourfourtwo-no10]
Numbered shirts reached English league football on 25 August 1928, in matches played by Arsenal and Chelsea. [src-premierleague-coachesvoice-no6-explained#numbers-trialled-1928-in-2-3-5][src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football#first-english-numbers-1928]
That was a landmark for England, not a world first, because numbered shirts were already recorded in Australia and Argentina. [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football#first-english-numbers-1928]
In the 2-3-5 the team sheet ran from goalkeeper to forwards: 1 goalkeeper, 2 and 3 full-backs, 4, 5 and 6 half-backs, 7 to 11 forwards. [src-premierleague-coachesvoice-no6-explained#numbers-trialled-1928-in-2-3-5][src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football]
The Football League made numbered shirts compulsory from the 1939-40 season. [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football#football-league-compulsory-1939][src-lcfc-squad-numbers-history]
When back lines changed, numbers kept roughly their 2-3-5 meaning and moved with the role, so the old centre-half number 5 followed that player back into central defence. [src-premierleague-coachesvoice-no6-explained][src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football]
The FA moved to persistent squad numbers in 1993 instead of 1 to 11 for the starting line-up. [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football#fa-persistent-numbers-1993]
The Premier League introduced squad numbers in 1993/94, its second season, after both 1992/93 cup finals used them. [src-lcfc-squad-numbers-history#premier-league-1993-94]
The first cup-final numbers did not follow playing position: Arsenal's suspended right-back Lee Dixon still got the 2, and John Jensen, who had worn the 7, was given the 17. [src-museumofjerseys-birth-of-squad-numbers-part-2]
Some clubs fell back to 1-11 lineups during 1993/94, and Arsenal did so in the second half of that season. [src-squadnumbers-1-11-premier-league-era][src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football]
Premier League rule M.1: before each season a club gives every first-team squad member a different shirt number. [src-premierleague-handbook-2026-27#m1-different-number-each]
Rule M.2 gives a number to anyone who joins the first-team squad during the season. [src-premierleague-handbook-2026-27#m2-in-season-joiners]
Rule M.3: unless the Board consents in writing, numbers start at one and run consecutively. [src-premierleague-handbook-2026-27#m3-start-at-one-consecutive]
Rule M.4: a player keeps their number for the whole season while they stay with the club. [src-premierleague-handbook-2026-27#m4-keep-number-all-season]
Rule M.5: when a player leaves, their number may be given to another player. [src-premierleague-handbook-2026-27#m5-number-reallocation]
Rule M.7: in league matches the shirt back shows the number clearly with the player's surname above it. [src-premierleague-handbook-2026-27#m7-number-on-back]
Rule M.8: the number also appears on the front of one leg of the shorts. [src-premierleague-handbook-2026-27#m8-number-on-shorts]
The 2026/27 Handbook rule on goalkeepers is about kit colour, and Section M as read does not reserve a number for the goalkeeper. [src-premierleague-handbook-2026-27#m10-goalkeeper-kit]
By convention the first-choice goalkeeper wears 1 and a second goalkeeper often wears 12 or 13. [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football]
StatsBomb labels positions by side and depth, with codes such as GK, RB, RCB, CB, LCB, LB, RWB and LWB, and every event stores the position held at that moment. [src-statsbomb-open-events-spec-v4#tactical-positions-guide][src-statsbomb-open-data]
kloppy groups provider positions into one standard tree, so a left back sits under full back, which sits under defender, and a player's position can change during the match. [src-kloppy-positions-docs]
The Wyscout public players table stores one main role per player as a name plus two abbreviations, which makes it a coarse label rather than a tactical role. [src-pappalardo-wyscout-dataset-paper#wyscout-player-role-field][src-wyscout-public-dataset]

## Misconceptions

A shirt number does not tell you the role: squad numbers today often differ from the position a player is deployed in. [src-fourfourtwo-no10#ten-traditionally-attacking-mid][src-goal-squad-numbers-explained]
At Liverpool the 7 was long linked to the attacking midfielder or second striker, not the right winger. [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football]
A formation such as 4-4-2 does not fix players to spots, because roles change what a player does from the same starting position. [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions#positions-not-rigid][src-fourfourtwo-inverted-full-back]
The number 10 became famous partly by accident: Brazil forgot to send its 1958 list and an official assigned numbers at random, giving Pelé the 10. [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football#pele-1958-random-ten]
The goalkeeper does not always wear 1: Chievo's Cristiano Lupatelli wore the 10 from 2001 to 2003. [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football]

## Contested points

Contested: what the 5 and the 6 mean. For England's traditional 4-4-2 the 5 and 6 are the two centre-backs and the 4 is the defensive midfielder [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football#england-442-numbering], while Coaches' Voice says the 6 usually goes to the deepest central midfielder and some countries and coaches use the 4 for that job [src-premierleague-coachesvoice-no6-explained#some-use-four-for-deepest-mid]; Goal says the 6 is commonly a centre-back such as Bobby Moore but increasingly a defensive midfielder [src-goal-squad-numbers-explained#six-centre-back-moore].
Contested: South American usage differs again, with Argentina's 5 as the holding midfielder and the 6 as the second centre-back [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football#argentina-five-holding-mid][src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football#argentina-six-stopper], and Brazil's 5 as the first volante, a defensive midfielder [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football#brazil-five-volante]; the English-centred explainers do not mention this scheme [src-premierleague-coachesvoice-no6-explained][src-goal-squad-numbers-explained].
Contested: where the 10 plays. In England's traditional 4-4-2 the 10 is the second striker and in Brazil's scheme it is a midfielder [src-wikipedia-squad-number-association-football], while FourFourTwo says the 10 traditionally went to the most attacking midfielder and is now an attacking-midfield position [src-fourfourtwo-no10#ten-traditionally-attacking-mid][src-bundesliga-positions-explained].
Contested: whether wingers are forwards or midfielders. Latin and Dutch football cultures count them as forwards and English-speaking areas usually count them as midfielders [src-wikipedia-association-football-positions], and the Bundesliga explainer lists the winger among the widest attacking positions [src-bundesliga-positions-explained].
