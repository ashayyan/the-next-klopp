# How analysts watch: visual ideas

## One-player spotlight (Show, then Touch)

A short wide-view clip dims everyone except one chosen player, for example the left-back, with a faint trail of his path. The learner replays it and drags a marker to where he went when the team lost the ball. Source idea: follow one player for a spell. [src-breakingthelines-watch-space]

## Question first, clip second (Practise)

Each clip opens with one question card, such as where is the space, who presses first, or which shape. The clip then plays with no hints, and the learner answers on a pitch panel by tapping. Source idea: pretests reduce mind wandering. [src-jarmac-pan-pretesting-video]

## Freeze and predict (Practise)

The clip freezes just before a key action. The learner taps where the ball goes next, then sees the real outcome and a sentence on the cue they missed. Source idea: occlusion training. [src-springer-temporal-occlusion-meta]

## Wide view versus broadcast view (Show)

A split-screen of the same moment: the broadcast cut on the left, a wide tactical frame on the right, with off-screen players drawn as ghost dots on the left. The learner toggles between them and counts who they could not see. Source idea: the tactical feed keeps the outfield players in shot. [src-arxiv-crang-broadcast-tracking]

## Phase tagger (Touch)

A strip of four buttons: attack, defend, and the two transitions. The learner taps the current phase while a clip plays, then compares their strip with the answer strip underneath. Source idea: four phases of play. [src-fourfourtwo-watch-like-analyst]

## Session meter that ends early (Reflect)

A small bar fills as clips are done and ends the session while the learner still wants more, with a choice to add one more clip. Source idea: keep sessions short and let the learner lengthen them. [src-acm-guo-mooc-video-engagement][src-aps-bradbury-attention-span]

## Live-ball share strip (Show)

A thin timeline of a match shaded by ball in play, with the dead time greyed out, so the learner sees why skipping to clips loses little. Source idea: ball-in-play time shrinks over a match. [src-jssm-linke-effective-playing-time]
