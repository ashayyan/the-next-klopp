# Formations: visual ideas for Tactics Board v1

## Two phases, one XI (Lesson Loop: Show, then Touch)

Put one XI on the pitch and give it a possession toggle. With the ball the shape morphs to a 3-2-4-1, without it to a 4-4-2, using Manchester City's 2022/23 Stones role as the worked case. [src-theanalyst-stones-hybrid-role] Players glide along curved paths while the label under the pitch swaps live. A second preset shows Arsenal sliding to a 2-3-5 when full-backs step inside. [src-skysports-arteta-tactics-2023] The learner sees the notation change without the people changing.

## Drag a shape, read the number (Lesson Loop: Touch)

The learner drags ten outfield discs (goalkeeper locked) and the board names the shape live by grouping players into lines from the back. A pulse on each line shows which digit it feeds, so 4-2-3-1 appears as four, two, three, one. [src-fourfourtwo-formations-in-football] Arrow keys nudge a selected disc. The target card asks for a given formation and shows a nearest-match score.

## Notation decoder (Lesson Loop: Show)

Tap a digit and its line lights up from the back. A toggle swaps 4-3-3 for a box midfield to show that the same numbers can hide different structures. [src-pl-box-midfield-explained] Step through 4-4-2, 4-3-3, 4-2-3-1, 3-5-2 and 3-4-3 as ghosted outlines laid over each other so differences read at a glance. [src-coachesvoice-formations-explained]

## Where does the space appear? (Lesson Loop: Practise)

Pick a formation for the opponent and the board paints the zones that tend to open: behind advancing full-backs for a 4-3-3, between the two lines of a flat 4-4-2, wide midfield for a back five. [src-coachesvoice-formations-explained][src-pl-442-explained] The learner taps the zone, gets instant feedback, then switches formation and sees the zone move. Final item: choose a formation to attack a 3-5-2. [src-pl-352-explained]

## From data to a shape (Lesson Loop: Touch, then Practise)

Load a StatsBomb Starting XI event, read the formation string and draw the lineup from the position ids. [src-statsbomb-events-spec-v4] Then overlay the average position of each player's events and add a window slider. The shape visibly compresses as the window grows, which teaches why averages mislead. [src-frontiers-formation-id-survey][src-mplsoccer-plot-formations] A final prompt asks the learner to spot where two players swapped places.

## Two labels, one build-up (Lesson Loop: Show)

Show a build-up frame with a toggle between two labels from different writers, such as 2-3-5 and a 3-2 base, and let the learner decide which fits. [src-skysports-arteta-tactics-2023] A side panel presents the telephone-numbers argument against the view that defensive structure still matters. [src-planetfootball-formations-whiteboard][src-yahoo-keble-formations-2016] Both sides stay visible; no verdict is forced.

## Back three that becomes a five (Lesson Loop: Practise)

Animate Conte's 2016/17 Chelsea as a back three in possession and a back five without the ball. [src-skysports-conte-343-2017] The learner scrubs a timeline, pauses and drags the wing-backs to the correct positions for each phase. A wrong answer ghosts the correct spot and explains the gap it leaves. This feeds the later Tactics Board animator.
